# Academic research into missed medical appointments

Academic research into missed medical appointments has evolved from viewing "no-shows" as simple patient irresponsibility to recognizing them as indicators of systemic socioeconomic barriers and clinical vulnerability.

The following key themes, data points, and insights are derived from major systematic reviews and cohort studies:

1. Global Prevalences and Risk Demographics

A comprehensive systematic review published in ScienceDirect found a global average appointment no-show rate of 23%, ranging significantly by region—from 13.2% in Oceania to 43% in Africa. [1] (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168851018300459)
According to a major systematic review in the British Journal of General Practice (BJGP), the strongest demographic predictors of missing appointments are: [1] (https://bjgp.org/content/71/707/e406)

- Age: Younger adults (under 21) and very elderly individuals (over 75). [1] (https://bjgp.org/content/71/707/e406)
- Socioeconomic Status: Patients living in high areas of deprivation or experiencing socioeconomic instability (unstable work hours, social isolation). [1] (https://bjgpopen.org/content/bjgpoa/early/2024/05/01/BJGPO.2024.0007.full.pdf), [2] (https://bjgp.org/content/71/707/e406)
- Clinical Profiles: Individuals diagnosed with mental health conditions, multi-morbidity, or a historical pattern of prior missed appointments. [1] (https://scholarworks.waldenu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=19066&context=dissertations), [2] (https://bjgp.org/content/71/707/e406)

2. The Critical "Missingness" and Mortality Link

One of the most profound shifts in recent academic literature is framing chronic non-attendance as a warning sign for severe clinical risk rather than administrative waste. [1] (https://bjgpopen.org/content/bjgpoa/early/2024/05/01/BJGPO.2024.0007.full.pdf)

- The Serial Missed Appointments Study: Published in BMC Medicine and highlighted by the University of Aberdeen, researchers discovered that patients who missed more than two appointments per year over a three-year period experienced a substantially higher annual mortality rate (around 5%), independent of their number of long-term conditions. [1] (https://www.abdn.ac.uk/iahs/academic/crh/projects/serial-missed-appointments-study/)
- Mental Health Vulnerability: This premature mortality risk was highest among individuals with mental health conditions who lacked recorded physical illnesses. Their deaths were predominantly caused by non-natural external factors, such as suicide, suggesting that missing appointments is a strong behavioural proxy for a patient in acute crisis. [1] (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253163)

3. Structural Causes vs. "Patient-Blaming" Models

A framework analysis published in NIHR Open Research challenges the classical administrative view that labels missed appointments as "service waste" caused by irresponsible individuals. Instead, the researchers introduce the "Missingness Lens," which demonstrates that non-attendance is routinely driven by complex structural inequalities, economic marginalisation, and system-level friction (such as excessive booking lead times and rigid scheduling interfaces). [1] (https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/14/5/623), [2] (https://openresearch.nihr.ac.uk/articles/6-23), [3] (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168851018300459)

4. Evidence-Based Interventions

A rapid systematic review available via PubMed evaluated the efficacy of combining data-driven predictive models with proactive interventions: [1] (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36508503/)

| Intervention Type                   | Clinical Evidence Impact                                              | Certainty Level      |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Predictive SMS Reminders            | Reduces no-shows by roughly 9% (Median RR 0.91)                       | High Certainty       |
| Predictive Phone-Call Reminders     | Reduces no-shows by roughly 39% (Median RR 0.61)                      | Moderate Certainty   |
| Patient Navigators (Human Outreach) | Reduces no-shows by roughly 45% (Median RR 0.55)                      | Moderate Certainty   |
| Telehealth Integration              | Can reduce no-shows by up to 29% in vulnerable safety-net populations | Observational Cohort |

Note: While behavioural economic interventions like text reminders effectively solve basic forgetfulness, a meta-analysis in PMC showed that financial penalties or punitive measures have highly limited efficacy and risk worsening healthcare inequalities for marginalized patients. [1] (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10594857/), [2] (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253163)
