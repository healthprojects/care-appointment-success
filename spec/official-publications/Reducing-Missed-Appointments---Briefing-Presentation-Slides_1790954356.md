## Slide 4: What Contracts for Innovation Cymru does

- Working across sectors to identify unmet needs.
- Supporting public sector to develop challenges, with an emphasis on relevance to Welsh businesses.
- Work with funding providers to secure funds for challenges, to enable 100% funding for R&D.

- Provide recognised training at a reduced cost, for example PRINCE2.

## Slide 9: Contracts for Innovation

## Slide 12: Contracts for Innovation key features

**Contracts**

- 100% funded R&D (procurement contract for R&D services).
- Payments made on completion of milestones.
- Costs quoted must reflect actual costs and not include profit.
- Contract with a single organisation, which may choose to sub-contract but remains accountable.

**IP rests with the supplier**

- Certain usage rights for the public sector (licences and so on).

**Eligibility**

- Can be used to adapt technology or processes from other sectors, as long as the activity is at least 50% research and development.
- Open to all organisations. There is no limit on the size or type of company.
- All organisations must demonstrate a route to market.

## Slide 19: Reducing missed appointments and improving access to planned care

An open innovation challenge to understand and address the communication, practical, behavioural, accessibility and service barriers that affect outpatient attendance.

Cwm Taf Morgannwg University Health Board (CTM), in partnership with Contracts for Innovation Cymru.

Supplier briefing, 2 October 2026.

## Slide 20: Case for change. Why this matters now

The figures describe the scale. Phase 1 should refine baselines, variation and causal understanding.

| Figure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 8.6% | Average outpatient did-not-attend (DNA) rate |
| 64k | Approximate missed appointments |
| £9.6m | Estimated annual lost value |
| 82k | People on outpatient waiting lists |
| 21.3% | Households without car or van |

- Each missed appointment is a lost opportunity for care and can delay diagnosis, treatment or monitoring.
- Unused clinic time reduces productivity while demand and waiting lists remain high.
- Headline improvement must be assessed alongside equity, experience and unintended consequences.

## Slide 21: Understanding the problem. Why people miss outpatient appointments

Non-attendance is an outcome of interacting patient, communication, practical and service factors.

[Diagram: "Why patients miss outpatient appointments. Causes of DNAs, mapped from what is to what is outside individuals' control." A tree under "Missed outpatient appointment (DNA)", with the colour key communication, practical barriers and service design.]

- **Within individuals' control**
  - Forgot the appointment
  - Felt it was no longer needed
- **Outside individuals' control**
  - Communication
    - Unaware of the appointment
    - Unclear or wrong information
    - Trouble cancelling or rearranging
    - Booked long ago, no reminder
  - Practical barriers
    - Can't get time off work
    - Transport issues
    - Difficulty arranging carers
    - Cost of attending
    - Not brought by a carer
  - Service design
    - Limited clinic hours

Applicants should show how discovery work will distinguish modifiable barriers from factors outside the intervention's control.

## Slide 22: Access and place. The same appointment can create very different barriers

Travel time, service location, appointment timing and household circumstances interact.

[Two maps of the CTM area. Left, labelled "45-minute drive reach": a map showing the area reachable within a 45-minute drive. Right, labelled "Households without access to a car or van": a map of Middle Layer Super Output Areas (MSOAs) shaded by the share of households with no car or van, with a legend for England and Wales.]

Transport and accessibility barriers are in scope. Projects focused primarily on providing, commissioning or subsidising transport are outside the intended scope.

## Slide 23: Existing landscape. Build on existing transformation, do not duplicate it

The challenge should complement current patient-contact, outpatient and NHS Wales digital developments.

| Existing context | What this challenge adds | Supplier implication |
|---|---|---|
| Patient Centred Contact Transformation | Understanding why people miss appointments | Explain how the proposal complements existing capabilities |
| Evolving outpatient contact and communication arrangements | Inclusive patient and staff co-design | Avoid assuming a blank digital landscape |
| NHS Wales App development | Targeted discovery across populations and settings | Identify dependencies early |
| Established NHS Wales patient administration infrastructure | Feasibility testing and evidence for adoption | Design for both digital and non-digital access |

Phase 1 is a test-and-learn route before any decision about wider deployment or adoption.

## Slide 24: Digital environment. Interoperability: proportionate to the Phase 1 proposal

Applicants need to distinguish what must be integrated now from what must be capable of integration later.

1. **Prototype safely.** Use fictional, synthetic or appropriately governed data where suitable for early design and testing.
2. **Describe dependencies.** Specify systems, data fields, APIs, workflows and technical support genuinely required in Phase 1.
3. **Plan future fit.** Explain how the concept could integrate with NHS Wales administrative and clinical systems if it progresses.

Working assumption: live integration is not a universal Phase 1 requirement. Requirements will depend on the proposed solution and will be agreed during mobilisation.

## Slide 25: Phase 1. Discover, design, test and learn

Phase 1 should generate credible learning, not begin with full enterprise deployment.

1. **Discover:** analyse patterns, map workflows and understand variation by service, place and population.
2. **Design:** co-design inclusive approaches with patients, booking teams, clinicians and partners.
3. **Test:** develop prototypes and undertake proportionate usability or feasibility testing.
4. **Learn:** assess acceptability, feasibility, safety, equity, value, scalability and future integration.

The strongest proposals will connect an explicit theory of change to measurable, equitable outcomes.

## Slide 26: Inclusive innovation. Who should shape the solution?

Recruitment and access arrangements will be agreed during mobilisation, subject to capacity, consent and governance.

- Booking and administrative staff
- Relevant clinicians and service managers
- Patients with planned-care experience
- Welsh speakers
- People with limited digital access or confidence
- Disabled people and those with communication needs
- People facing transport, work, caring or financial barriers
- Communities experiencing poorer access or higher deprivation

Design for varied levels of digital access, health literacy, language need and accessibility from the outset.

## Slide 27: Evidence and value. How we will recognise useful learning

Success is broader than a single aggregate DNA percentage.

| Patients and communities | Staff and services | System and adoption |
|---|---|---|
| Attendance and cancellation outcomes | Clinic utilisation | Cost, value and sustainability |
| Successful appointment communication | Booking and administrative workload | Safety and unintended consequences |
| Experience and ease of access | Workflow fit and usability | Scalability and affordability |
| Differential effects by population group and geography | Waiting-time or capacity implications | Future integration and adoption requirements |

Application prompt: What change will your approach produce, for whom, through what mechanism, and how will you know?

## Slide 28: Assurance. Governance, approvals and clinical safety

Requirements will be proportionate to the proposed activities, data use and risk profile.

- **Evaluation and research**
  - CfIC (Contracts for Innovation Cymru) will determine the appropriate evaluation or research route.
  - Applicants should clearly describe participants, methods, data use and intended generalisability.
- **Information governance**
  - Data minimisation, privacy, transparency and secure handling are expected from the start.
  - A relevant information governance (IG) route will be identified during mobilisation.
- **Digital assurance**
  - Cybersecurity and technical assurance will reflect the maturity, intended use and risk profile of the proposed approach.
- **Clinical safety**
  - Suppliers proposing solutions should identify applicable clinical-safety duties, hazards, documentation and responsibilities.

Items still to be confirmed through the formal competition route: approval timescales, local Clinical Safety Officer arrangements and the named information-governance contact.

## Slide 29: Partnership offer. What CTM anticipates facilitating

Support will be proportionate and subject to project needs, capacity and relevant approvals.

| CTM anticipates facilitating | Applicants should specify |
|---|---|
| Access to relevant operational and clinical stakeholders | People, services and staff time required |
| Engagement with booking and administrative teams | Data fields and level of detail required |
| Appropriate patient and public involvement routes | Technical dependencies and test environment |
| Appropriately anonymised or aggregated attendance information | Patient recruitment requirements |
| Digital, analytics, IG and clinical-safety discussions | Approval and safety documentation |
| Understanding of current workflows and feasible test environments | What remains deliverable if some dependencies are unavailable |

Be specific early: clear dependencies make a Phase 1 proposal easier to assess, mobilise and govern.

## Slide 30: Closing. What a strong application will show

Innovation is valuable when it addresses a real barrier, can be tested safely and produces credible evidence.

1. A credible understanding of the access problem.
2. A feasible Phase 1, not a full-scale deployment.
3. Meaningful patient and staff co-design.
4. Welsh language, accessibility and digital inclusion.
5. Clear outcomes and an evaluation approach.
6. Transparent data, integration, governance and safety needs.
7. Scalability, affordability and potential adoption.
8. Complementarity with existing transformation.

We are looking for partners who can understand the problem precisely, work with patients and staff, test promising approaches safely, and show what could improve attendance and equitable access.
