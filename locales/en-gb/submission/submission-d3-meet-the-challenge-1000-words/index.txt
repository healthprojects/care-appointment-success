The challenge. is 64,000 missed outpatient appointments a year, a "did not attend" (DNA) rate of 8.6%, and about £9.6 million of avoidable lost value, or roughly £150 each. It implies about 744,000 outpatient appointments a year, so every percentage point off the DNA rate frees about 7,400 appointments, worth about £1.1 million. These are estimates, not cashable savings.

The health board's own review found that people miss appointments because barriers get in the way, and the barriers interact: late or unclear communication, transport and distance, deprivation, digital exclusion, caring and work, and accessibility needs. A reminder fixes at most one of them.

Our answer: four steps for every appointment.

1. Ask.

- A clear reminder in Welsh or English, by text, voice call, email or letter, in the person's chosen language and format.

- Easy-read, large print, audio and British Sign Language versions are available.

- People answer in one tap or one word: confirm, cancel or choose another time.

- Silent patients are not forgotten. The next reminder uses another channel, and the last step is a phone call from staff.

2. Help.

- A short "what's in the way?" question links each reason to help that already exists: the Healthcare Travel Costs Scheme, community transport, carer services, employer letters, reasonable adjustments, a different time, or a phone or video appointment where a clinician has approved it for that type of clinic.

- People who want a person are phoned by a community navigator from a local voluntary-sector partner, in Welsh or English.

- We signpost transport. We do not fund or provide it.

3. Hand back. Early cancellations are offered to people on the waiting list, so freed time becomes care delivered.

4. Learn. We report attendance, refilled slots and experience by language and by deprivation quintile.

A fair, explainable tool helps staff choose who to phone first. It can only add support. It never overbooks, deprioritises or penalises anyone.

Current state of development.

- Our open-source reference system, the Patient Administration System (PAS) prototyper with fictitious information, contains the engine: two-way replies in Welsh and English, a reminder sequence that escalates to a phone call, safe reply links, opt-out and quiet hours, released-slot offers and privacy-protected reporting. It has automated tests. It has never sent a real message or held real patient data. It is not the Welsh Patient Administration System (WPAS).

- A bilingual web prototype shows the patient journey with fictional appointments.

- Not yet done: testing with patients, the health board's data, and a route into WPAS. Phase 1 does these.

Evidence for the approach.

- Reminders help modestly. A Cochrane review found text reminders beat no reminder or post and match phone calls, on low to moderate quality evidence. NHS England (2023) treats reminders and easy cancelling as the baseline, so they are our starting point, not our innovation.

- Wording matters. In two randomised trials, stating the cost of a missed appointment cut misses from 11.1% to 8.4% (Hallsworth and others, 2015). We will test wording separately in Welsh and English, with results by deprivation, because it can read as blame.

- Booking matters. One general-practice study found lead time the strongest predictor of a miss, and Scottish guidance links patient-choice booking to fewer misses. We will test both in outpatients.

- Fairness matters. A US study found that overbooking on no-show scores made Black patients wait about 30% longer. This is why our score only adds support.

- The gap. The NHS Wales App shows hospital appointments but cannot cancel or rebook them. We supply the two-way step.

- Navigators are a hypothesis. We found no published evidence that they reduce missed appointments, so Phase 1 measures whether they reach people, resolve the barrier and whether the person attends.

Phase 1 (November 2026 to March 2027). No live patient messages are sent.

- Discovery: analyse de-identified appointment data for where misses concentrate, and set baselines.

- Co-design: interviews and bilingual workshops with patients, carers, disabled people, Welsh speakers and digitally excluded people, plus booking staff and clinicians.

- Prototype: the bilingual ask-and-help flow, accessible formats and the navigator pathway.

- Validation: the support tool tested on historical data with a fairness audit, and an integration assessment with Digital Health and Care Wales (DHCW).

- Testing: at least 30 patients, at least 10 of them Welsh speakers.

- Feasibility: a costed service design and a Phase 2 evaluation plan.

Benefits, including for households and businesses in Wales.

- Patients and families: earlier care, fewer wasted journeys, less lost work and caring time, and communication in their own language.

- Staff: fuller clinics, less chasing and rebooking.

- Health board and NHS Wales: lower DNA rates, shorter waits and a narrower gap between the most and least deprived areas.

- Welsh households and businesses: less time off work, and paid work for Welsh voluntary-sector and language partners.

Measures: DNA rate, the deprivation gap, Welsh and English parity, early cancellation and slot reuse, patient experience, staff time and cost per missed appointment avoided.

Scale and safety.

- The service is modular and uses open standards. The reference code is free to use, so any health board can adopt a part or all of it without lock-in.

- Clinical oversight stays. Urgent appointments always get human follow-up. A patient's cancel on an urgent pathway is held and a person phones first.

- Collaboration: the health board's planned care, outpatient, information governance, Welsh language and patient experience teams; a voluntary-sector partner; a Welsh-language partner; and [an academic evaluation partner]. We will share learning with other health boards.
