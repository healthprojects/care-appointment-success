What the money buys:

- Staff time (68 days, £34,000) delivers discovery and baseline analysis, the tested bilingual flow, the fairness audit and the integration assessment. Because the reference software already exists and is free, the days go on testing and adapting it with people and data, not on building from nothing.
- The Welsh-language partner (£4,000) and accessible formats (£2,500) deliver the Welsh and English parity and accessible formats the challenge requires.
- The voluntary-sector partner (£6,000) and participant payments and venues (£2,000) reach seldom-heard patients and pay them fairly.
- Travel (£1,000) covers workshops and testing in the health board area.
- Nothing is spent on transport provision or subsidy, which the challenge excludes.

Value for money for the taxpayer:

- Avoiding about 400 missed appointments at about £150 of lost value each repays the whole of Phase 1. That is about 0.6% of one year's roughly 64,000 missed appointments.
- Phase 1 is small and low-risk: fictional appointments, free open-source software, and two go/no-go gates, so the funder commits to Phase 2 only on evidence.
- About 69% of the cost is delivery staff time and about 20% is subcontracts, mostly fixed price, so cost is predictable.
- The request is a modest share of the £200,000 portfolio, which leaves room for complementary projects.
- Payment is on completed milestones only, with 20% held until the closure report. We ask for nothing in advance.
- The outputs, including the affordability model and the free reference software, are designed to be reused across Wales, so other health boards benefit without paying for the same work twice.

Value for money for us: Phase 1 gives us the evidence to decide on Phase 2 and on adoption across Wales, at a cost we can bear without taking profit.
