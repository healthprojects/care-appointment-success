How we will assure the solution is fit for purpose. Every test has a pass criterion agreed before the results are seen. Results feed the Gate 2 go/no-go decision in February 2027 and the final report. Phase 1 uses fictional appointments and sends no live patient messages.

Testing requirements:

1. Usability.
   - Moderated tests with at least 30 patients, at least 10 of them Welsh speakers. They include disabled people, carers, older people and digitally excluded people.
   - Tasks are completed in each language and across text message, voice call and letter.
   - Pass: at least 80% task completion, with no significant gap between Welsh and English.
2. Accessibility.
   - Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 level AA for the digital parts.
   - The Accessible Communication and Information Standards.
   - Easy-read, large-print and audio versions tested with users.
   - Screen reader and assistive technology checks.
3. Welsh language.
   - Review by first-language speakers.
   - Automated checks that block any content existing in only one language.
   - Active Offer checks at every point of contact.
4. Risk model.
   - Validation on held-out historical data, with calibration and accuracy measures.
   - A fairness audit of error rates by deprivation quintile, age, sex and language preference.
   - Pass: no group outside agreed error bounds, and no score ever reduces the contact or slots a patient is offered.
   - A model card that documents its limits.
5. Data quality. We measure the share of patients with no usable mobile number and the message bounce rate, because stale contact details limit every reminder.
6. Integration. We test reading appointments, writing back cancellations and avoiding duplicate messages against test data, and agree the result with the health board's digital team and Digital Health and Care Wales.
7. Clinical safety.
   - A hazard log started in month 1, following the NHS clinical risk management standards (DCB0129 and DCB0160).
   - A clinician-approved list of appointments that must always receive human follow-up.
8. Security and data protection.
   - Secure development practices and automated tests.
   - A Digital Technology Assessment Criteria self-assessment.
   - A data protection impact assessment drafted with the health board's information governance team.
   - A penetration test planned before any live use in Phase 2.

Evaluation process:

- The test plan is agreed with the challenge lead at the first milestone, so success is judged on criteria set in advance.
- Testing runs alongside build. Usability findings from each round are fixed and retested before the next round.
- At Gate 2 we report results against every criterion. Anything that fails is either fixed or removed from the Phase 2 recommendation, so the funder only hears a "go" for components that passed.
- The final report states results against each criterion, what changed as a result, the limits of Phase 1 evidence, and the conditions for Phase 2.
- We also design the Phase 2 real-world evaluation. It compares matched clinics, or uses a phased rollout, and measures missed appointment rate, slots refilled, cost per 1,000 appointments, and journeys and clinic sessions saved. It reports separately for Welsh speakers and deprived areas, so we can see whether the gap is narrowing.
- Results are published in plain language, in Welsh and English.

Specialist support:

- Academic evaluation partner. We will subcontract an academic statistician or health services researcher to review the model validation and fairness audit, and to co-design the Phase 2 evaluation. They will review the analysis plan before results are seen, and sign off the methods in the final report. Their work is covered by the subcontract terms described in section E1, so we keep freedom to operate.
- Clinical safety officer. A qualified clinician, subcontracted or provided through the health board, will own the hazard log and approve the urgent-appointment list.
- Welsh-language partner. First-language speakers review all content and run the Welsh user tests.
- Voluntary-sector partner. They recruit seldom-heard participants and host sessions in community venues.
- Health board advisers. Clinical, information governance and digital advisers, offered as challenge-partner support, review safety, data protection and integration findings.
