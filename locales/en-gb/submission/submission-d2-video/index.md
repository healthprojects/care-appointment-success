Video script, 3 minutes maximum (about 370 spoken words). Record it twice, once with Welsh narration and Welsh captions and once with English narration and English captions, or record one version with both languages spoken. Use fictional data only on screen, and say so in the video. Text in [square brackets] is a choice for the presenter.

0:00 to 0:20. The problem. (On screen: a calendar with empty clinic slots.)

"Every year about 64,000 outpatient appointments in Cwm Taf Morgannwg are missed. That is one in twelve. Each one is a person who did not get care, and a slot that could have helped someone else. The health board estimates the lost value at 9.6 million pounds a year."

0:20 to 0:50. The reason. (On screen: five short fictional scenes: a late letter, a missing bus, a carer, a shift worker, an English-only message.)

"People rarely choose to miss. A letter comes late. There is no bus. There is a child to collect, a shift that cannot be swapped, a message in the wrong language. A reminder fixes none of these."

0:50 to 1:40. The idea. (On screen: the fictional patient journey in the prototype, in Welsh, then English.)

"Care Appointment Success does four things. It asks: a clear reminder in Welsh or English, answered in one tap. It helps: if you cannot come, one question finds out why, and points you to help that exists, such as travel cost refunds, carer support, another time, or a phone or video appointment if your clinician agrees. If you want a person, a community navigator phones you. It hands back: the slot you release goes to someone on the waiting list. And it learns: we measure what works, split by language and by area, so that no group is left behind."

1:40 to 2:10. Fairness and safety. (On screen: the staff view with reasons in words.)

"Behind the scenes, a transparent tool helps staff choose who to phone first. It can only add support. It never overbooks, never penalises, and never reduces anyone's appointments. Urgent appointments always get a human follow-up."

2:10 to 2:35. Why us. (On screen: the open-source repository and the bilingual prototype.)

"We have already built an open-source reference system that handles Welsh and English replies, reminders and released slots. In five months we will test it with patients, including at least ten Welsh speakers, and with the health board's data, and report what works. No real patient messages are sent in this phase."

2:35 to 3:00. The ask. (On screen: title card with the project name.)

"Fewer wasted appointments, shorter waits and fairer access. Fund Care Appointment Success, and we will show Wales what works. Diolch."
