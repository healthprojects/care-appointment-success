Yes. We will make the solution accessible, inclusive and available in Welsh and English throughout the project, in design and testing, and beyond. This fits the Well-being of Future Generations (Wales) Act 2015: it supports long-term, preventive access to care and treats the Welsh language as part of good design, not an add-on.

How we treat accessibility, Welsh language and equity together:

- Welsh and English are equal in the design. The same people, the same tests and the same pass criteria cover both.
- Accessibility needs and language preference are recorded once and respected on every channel.
- Our fairness audit looks at language, deprivation, age and sex together, so improvements for one group do not widen gaps for another.
- Patients, carers, Welsh speakers and health board staff help design and test it, through our voluntary-sector and Welsh-language partners.

Planned accessibility improvements during the project:

- Easy-read, large-print, audio and British Sign Language video versions of appointment messages and letters, co-designed and tested with users.
- Formats selected automatically from recorded communication needs, in line with the Accessible Communication and Information Standards.
- Voice calls, letters and community navigator phone calls for people who are digitally excluded, and plain-language content for everyone.
- Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 level AA for the digital parts, checked with screen readers and other assistive technology, and tested with disabled users.
- Usability tests that include disabled people, carers, older people and digitally excluded people.

How Welsh-speaking users will access and use the solution:

- Every patient-facing message, voice prompt, form, letter and piece of research material is written in Welsh and English from the start, never translated afterwards.
- The Welsh Language Active Offer is made proactively. Patients are asked their language preference up front, and it is used on every channel.
- First-language Welsh speakers review all content. Automated checks block any content that exists in only one language.
- Welsh characters such as ŵ and ŷ can change the text-message encoding and the message length. We test this early and set a message-length budget.
- Welsh text-to-speech quality varies, so where it is poor we use recorded Welsh voice prompts.
- Welsh-speaking community navigators are available by phone.
- At least 10 of our 30 usability test participants will be Welsh speakers, and Welsh–English parity is a pass criterion.
- Automated and changing content, including replies and any future artificial intelligence features, is rechecked for Welsh parity as the product evolves.

Beyond the project:

- Our Phase 2 evaluation design reports results separately for Welsh speakers.
- Accessibility and Welsh parity checks become part of every release.
- The content model keeps both languages equal, so new messages cannot go live in one language only.
