Yes. The solution will be accessible, inclusive and available in Welsh and English throughout the project, in design and testing, and beyond. This follows the Well-being of Future Generations (Wales) Act 2015: access to care is a long-term, preventive goal, and the Welsh language is part of good design, not an add-on.

One approach for language, accessibility and fairness:

- Welsh and English are equal in the design. The same people, tests and pass criteria cover both.
- A person's language and communication needs are recorded once and used on every channel and every message.
- Our fairness audit looks at language, deprivation, age and sex together, so improving things for one group does not widen the gap for another.
- Patients, carers, Welsh speakers, disabled people and staff help design and test it, through our voluntary-sector and Welsh-language partners.

Accessibility improvements during the project:

- Easy-read, large-print, audio and British Sign Language video versions of appointment messages and letters, co-designed and tested with users.
- Formats chosen automatically from recorded needs, in line with the Accessible Communication and Information Standards. A person who needs letters only gets letters only.
- Voice calls, letters and community navigator phone calls for people who are digitally excluded, and plain language for everyone.
- Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 level AA for the digital parts, checked with screen readers and other assistive technology, and with disabled users.
- Reply links that work with keyboards and screen readers, and a phone keypad option for people who cannot use a smartphone.
- Usability tests that include disabled people, carers, older people and digitally excluded people.

How Welsh speakers will use the solution:

- Every message, voice prompt, form, letter and piece of research material is written in Welsh and English from the start, never translated afterwards.
- The Welsh Language Active Offer is made proactively: we ask for language preference first and use it everywhere.
- First-language Welsh speakers review all content. The system refuses to activate any message that exists in only one language.
- Replies are understood in Welsh and English. Welsh characters such as ŵ and ŷ can change text-message length, so we test this early and set a message-length budget.
- Welsh text-to-speech quality varies, so where it is poor we use recorded Welsh voice prompts.
- Welsh-speaking navigators are available by phone.
- At least 10 of our 30 usability testers will be Welsh speakers, and Welsh and English parity is a pass criterion.
- Automated and changing content, including replies and any future artificial intelligence features, is rechecked for Welsh parity as the product changes.

Beyond the project:

- The Phase 2 evaluation reports results separately for Welsh speakers.
- Accessibility and Welsh parity checks run on every release.
- The content model keeps both languages equal, so a message cannot go live in one language only.

Welsh translation and review costs are included in section H.
