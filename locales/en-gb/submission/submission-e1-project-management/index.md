Plan in brief. A five-month plan (November 2026 to 31 March 2027) with five payable milestones and two go/no-go gates. Phase 1 sends no live patient messages.

Project management process:

- Fortnightly sprints, with a weekly team check-in.
- A monthly steering group: the health board's challenge lead, planned care, information governance, Welsh language and patient experience representatives, and our subcontractors.
- A fortnightly progress report and a weekly-reviewed log of risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies.
- Patients are involved at every stage through our voluntary-sector partner.

Resources:

- About 68 days of delivery time: project lead and service design, data science, and software engineering.
- Subcontractors for Welsh-language review, accessible formats and community engagement.
- Participant payments and community venues.
- Health board data access, plus clinical and information governance input offered as challenge-partner support.

Success criteria:

- At least 80% task completion in usability testing, in both Welsh and English, with no significant gap between languages.
- A well-calibrated risk model, with no group in the fairness audit outside agreed error bounds.
- A viable integration route agreed with the health board's digital team.
- A modelled benefit-to-cost ratio of at least 3:1.

Gates:

- Gate 1 (December). Go if data access is confirmed and discovery finds barriers we can address. Otherwise we use aggregate or synthetic data and narrow to the one or two highest-impact specialties.
- Gate 2 (February). Go to a Phase 2 recommendation if the success criteria are met. Otherwise we recommend only the components that passed.

Christmas has reduced activity. Information governance work runs alongside discovery.

Main risks and mitigation (full register in section E2):

- Technical: delayed data access. We submit the request in week 1, use challenge-partner governance support, and fall back on synthetic data at Gate 1.
- Technical: limits on integration with the Welsh Patient Administration System or the existing reminder supplier. We design to be integration-agnostic, engage Digital Health and Care Wales early, and keep a batch-file fallback.
- Technical: Welsh text-to-speech and text-message encoding limits. We test early and record Welsh prompts where needed.
- Technical: model bias. The model only triggers more support, never overbooking; the fairness audit is published.
- Commercial: affordability at scale, and overlap with existing suppliers. We model cost per 1,000 appointments, offer modular pricing, and keep open interfaces.
- Commercial: limited health board staff time. Short sessions are agreed at M1.
- Environmental: the footprint of hosting and letters. We are digital-first, use low-carbon UK hosting.

Intellectual property (IP):

- Before the start, background IP, including our own open-source patient administration system, is recorded in the contract's register (Schedule C). Project IP is logged monthly in the foreground IP register (Schedule B).
- Each subcontract assigns project IP to us, keeps the subcontractor's background IP with them under a licence to us, and flows down the contract's IP, confidentiality, data protection and audit terms. We can then grant the funder its royalty-free, non-exclusive UK licence.
- Freedom to operate: before we reuse any open-source component, dataset or translation, we check its licence, avoid terms that restrict commercial or public-sector use, and record it in an IP and dependency register. We copy nothing whose licence we cannot confirm.
- The health board's data stays its own; we only license its use.
- We seek the funder's approval before any IP assignment, and plan to exploit the IP within three years.
- Any IP issue goes to the project lead within two working days, then the steering group.
