The plan in one line. Five months (November 2026 to 31 March 2027), five payable milestones, two go/no-go gates. Phase 1 sends no live patient messages. A Gantt chart is attached (plan-gantt-chart.pdf).

How we run it:

- Fortnightly sprints, a weekly team check-in, and a risk, assumption, issue and dependency log reviewed weekly.
- A monthly steering group: the health board's challenge lead and its planned care, information governance, Welsh language and patient experience representatives, plus our subcontractors.
- A fortnightly progress note to the challenge lead.
- Patients help at every stage, through our voluntary-sector partner.
- About 68 delivery days: project lead and service design, data science, software engineering. Subcontracts cover Welsh-language review, and community engagement. Accessible formats, participant payments and venues are bought directly. The health board's data, clinical and governance input is offered as challenge-partner support.

Success criteria, set now and judged at Gate 2:

- At least 80% task completion in usability tests, in Welsh and English, with no significant gap between languages.
- A support tool that is well calibrated, with no group outside agreed error bounds in the fairness audit.
- An integration route agreed with the health board's digital team.
- A modelled benefit-to-cost ratio of at least 3:1.

Gates:

- Gate 1 (December): go if data access is confirmed and discovery finds barriers we can address. Otherwise we use aggregate or synthetic data and narrow to the one or two highest-impact specialties.
- Gate 2 (February): recommend Phase 2 if the success criteria are met. Otherwise we recommend only the components that passed.

Christmas reduces activity, so information governance runs alongside discovery. Main risks (full register in section E2): delayed data access, limits on integration with the Welsh Patient Administration System (WPAS), Welsh content quality, model bias, and affordability.

Intellectual property (IP):

- Before the start, background IP, including our own open-source patient administration system, is recorded in the contract's register (Schedule C). Project IP is logged monthly in the foreground IP register (Schedule B).
- Each subcontract assigns project IP to us, keeps the subcontractor's own background IP with them under a licence to us, and flows down the contract's IP, confidentiality, data protection and audit terms. We can then grant the funder its royalty-free, non-exclusive UK licence.
- Freedom to operate: before reusing any open-source component, dataset or translation, we check its licence, avoid terms that restrict public-sector use, and record it in a dependency register. We copy nothing whose licence we cannot confirm.
- The health board's data stays its own. We seek the funder's approval before any IP assignment, and plan to exploit the IP within three years.
- IP issues go to the project lead within two working days, then the steering group.
