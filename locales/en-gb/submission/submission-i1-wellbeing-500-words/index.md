Care Appointment Success helps Welsh Government improve long-term wellbeing by helping people get planned care, with the biggest gains aimed at those who now find it hardest to attend.

How the project supports the seven well-being goals of the Well-being of Future Generations (Wales) Act 2015:

| Goal | Example from this project | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| A healthier Wales | More people get timely planned care | Change in missed-appointment rate (modelled in Phase 1, measured in Phase 2) |
| A more equal Wales | Support targeted at deprived, disabled, caring and digitally excluded people | Missed-appointment gap between the most and least deprived areas, reported and never widened |
| A Wales of vibrant culture and thriving Welsh language | Welsh speakers get an equal experience | 100% of content in both languages; at least 10 of 30 usability testers are Welsh speakers; Welsh–English parity is a pass criterion |
| A Wales of cohesive communities | Voluntary-sector navigators link people to local support | Number of community organisations in the signposting directory |
| A prosperous Wales | Less time lost from work and caring through missed or repeated visits | Patient-reported time saved |
| A globally responsible Wales | Fewer wasted journeys and clinic sessions | Estimated journeys and carbon dioxide avoided |
| A resilient Wales | Reusable assets reduce duplicated effort across health boards | Number of assets published for NHS Wales |

How the project follows the five ways of working:

- Long term: the barrier taxonomy, content library and evaluation framework can be reused by every health board.
- Prevention: barriers are found and removed before an appointment is missed, and released slots help prevent longer waits.
- Integration: the service links health, council and voluntary-sector support, and fits with existing NHS Wales systems rather than replacing them.
- Collaboration: it is delivered with Welsh partners, the health board and community organisations.
- Involvement: patients, carers, Welsh speakers, disabled people and booking staff co-design and test it.

Fairness is built in. A risk model can only trigger more support. It never reduces appointments or contact, and its fairness audit by deprivation, age, sex and language is published.

These are targets that Phase 1 tests. Phase 1 does not send live patient messages, so population-level benefits would be measured in Phase 2.
